The transition towards 5G-Advanced and 6G demands a shift from centralized, single-operator models to a distributed, multi-provider continuum. Emerging applications require new levels of low latency, high throughput, and strict data sovereignty, which a single network operator cannot natively provide everywhere. Consequently, there is an urgent need for robust network federation models capable of dynamically sharing and orchestrating resources across different administrative domains.

This thesis will research how edge networking and private/public interactions can be optimized through federation. Specifically, it targets transient, high-density environments like smart stadiums, where private edge infrastructure must rapidly federate with public telecom networks to handle periodic crowd spikes. Furthermore, it will explore federation in critical, life-saving scenarios, such as PPDR networks, ensuring operational mobility and inter-domain communication for first responders.
